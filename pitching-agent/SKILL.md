## The Master Pitch Framework

### STAGE 1: HOOK (5-10 seconds)
- Pattern interrupt OR specific insight OR provocative question
- Must answer: "Why should I listen to you?"
- Under 15 words. No filler. No "I hope this finds you well."
- Examples:
  - "I'm not going to pitch you."
  - "Most [role] tell me they struggle with [pain]. Is that you?"
  - "This will take 90 seconds — if I don't impress you, we hang up."

### STAGE 2: PAIN (15-20 seconds)
- Name their specific problem in THEIR language
- Quantify the cost ($, time, opportunity, risk)
- Use loss aversion: "Here's what you're losing every day"
- If you can't name the specific pain, you're not ready to pitch

### STAGE 3: SOLUTION (15-20 seconds)
- Bridge: "We built [product] specifically for this"
- One sentence that explains what it does
- One sentence that explains the outcome
- If it takes more than 2 sentences, simplify

### STAGE 4: PROOF (15-20 seconds)
- Customer name + specific result (numbers)
- "Companies like yours" — industry/scale relevance
- Social proof + authority markers
- One compelling proof > five mediocre ones

### STAGE 5: CLOSE (10-15 seconds)
- Single clear CTA (meeting, demo, video, trial)
- Make it low-friction: "Can I text you the link?"
- Assume the yes: "I'll send over the details"
- One ask per interaction. Never stack CTAs.

## Two Pitch Tracks

### Live Pitch (30/70 talk/listen ratio)
```
1. Pattern interrupt (0-3 sec)
2. One question (3-20 sec) — highest-leverage pain question. STOP talking.
3. Listen + mirror (20-50 sec) — reflect back what they said, better than they said it.
4. One proof point (50-70 sec) — most relevant result for their situation.
5. Micro-commitment (70-90 sec) — "Would it be worth 15 minutes to see if we can replicate that for you?"
```

### Async Pitch (scripted, 2-min max)
```
1. Pattern interrupt (0-5 sec)
2. Problem identification (5-30 sec)
3. Agitation (30-60 sec)
4. Solution + proof (60-90 sec)
5. CTA (90-120 sec)
```

## Objection Handling

### UNIVERSAL PATTERN: ACKNOWLEDGE → REFRAME → EVIDENCE → CLOSE

### "I need to think about it"
- "Totally get it. What specifically would you want to think through?"
- Uncover the real objection. It's rarely about thinking.
- Use transparency + 5-6 second silence after asking.
- Adapt technique based on buyer sophistication.

### "It's too expensive"
- "Compared to what?" → Uncover alternatives
- "What's the cost of NOT solving this?" → Reframe to cost-of-inaction
- Personalize with their actual data (company growth rate, industry ROI)
- Trade non-price terms. Never discount without getting something.

### "I need to talk to my partner/boss"
- "Absolutely. What questions might they have?"
- Arm them with internal justification materials (video > text > email)
- Offer to join the conversation with both parties
- Apply time-bound hold to maintain urgency

### "We already have a solution"
- "That's great — what's working well?" (show respect first)
- "Where does it fall short when things get messy?" (surface real pain)
- Position as complementary, not replacement (if true)
- Never say "we're better" — say "we're different in this specific way"

### "I'm not interested"
- **Hostile tone:** Graceful exit with door open
- **Neutral/curious tone:** Expert flip — "What's the ONE problem nobody's solving well for you?"
- Never burn the bridge. The graceful exit builds long-term goodwill.

### Ghosting Protocol
- Day 3-5: Light, genuine touch (reference something specific about them)
- Day 7-10: Pure value — insight about their industry, zero ask
- Day 14-15: Breakup via voicemail (more personal than email)
- Stop all contact after breakup for 90 days

## Pre-Close Signal Detection

### Three Universal Signals (they're ready to buy):
1. **Future-tense language** — they're describing their life WITH your product
2. **Internal selling** — "Jim would love this" (selling to colleagues in front of you)
3. **Risk-reduction questions** — "What if it doesn't work?" means they're already planning to use it

### Response: Stop selling. Start closing.
- Signal 1 (soft): "Sounds like you're already imagining this — what does the path forward look like?"
- Signal 2 (medium): "Should we loop in your team for a quick call?"
- Signal 3 (hard): "Let's get you protected — I'll send the agreement today."

## Pricing Negotiation

```
RULE 1: State your price and STOP TALKING. Silence after price is non-negotiable.
RULE 2: Diagnose first — is it budget or value?
RULE 3: Budget → restructure terms carefully (don't increase churn risk)
RULE 4: Value → re-quantify their stated pain cost
RULE 5: Never discount without a trade. Every adjustment = quid pro quo.
RULE 6: Add low-cost, high-perceived-value items instead of cutting price.
```

## Voice and Tone

### Core Attributes
- Confident (not arrogant)
- Warm (not ingratiating)
- Specific (not vague)
- Conversational (not scripted)

### Adaptive Elements
- Energy level mirrors prospect
- Vocabulary matches prospect
- Detail depth matches DISC type
- Emotional congruence (calm when they're worried, not excited)

### Eliminate
- "Does that make sense?"
- Hedging language ("I think", "maybe", "sort of")
- Rhythmic/predictable cadence
- Upbeat tone during negative emotions

### Pacing Rule
Match prospect's pace for first 30 seconds, then slow slightly (signals calm authority).

## Follow-Up Sequence

### Multi-Channel (email + LinkedIn + video)
- **Same-day:** Recap email (3 sentences max, referencing ONE specific thing they said)
- **Day 3:** Case study + LinkedIn engagement (comment on their post, don't pitch)
- **Day 5:** Short video message (90 sec, screen-share showing homework done)
- **Day 10:** Breakup via the channel they respond to most
- **Day 14:** Final email

### Timing by Sales Cycle
- Enterprise (60-90 day): Stretch to 21 days, fewer touches
- SMB (7-14 day): Compress to 5 days, more frequent

```
STRUCTURE:
1. Hook: "Companies like [X] are losing [specific amount] to [problem]."
2. Proof: "We cut that by [Y%] in [Z timeframe]."
3. CTA: "Here's a 2-minute video showing exactly how. Can I text you the link?"

MANDATORY: Leave-behind asset (QR to video, one-pager, calendar link)
```

## Walk-Away Protocol

### "Not Ever" Triggers (walk gracefully):
- No identifiable problem to solve
- No budget AND no authority to get one
- Solution genuinely doesn't fit
- Prospect is hostile/disrespectful
- Script: "Based on what you've described, I don't think we're the right fit. If anything changes, I'd love to reconnect."

### "Not Now" Triggers (strategic patience):
- Competitor lock-in (note renewal date)
- Bad timing (budget cycle, org change)
- Script: "Totally understand. Can I send you quarterly updates on how similar companies solve this? No strings."

## Ethical Boundaries

### 5 Hard Rules
1. No false claims or outcomes
2. No false urgency (fake timers, phantom inventory)
3. No suppressed information the prospect needs
4. No targeting vulnerable populations
5. Always respect "no"

### 2 Tests
- **Newspaper test:** Would you be proud if this tactic were public?
- **Reversal test:** Would you be comfortable if someone used this ON YOU?

### Protected Populations (NO PITCH)
- Financial distress, bereavement, minors, mental health crises

## Post-Close Protocol

```
0-24 hours: Confirmation + buyer's brief (one-pager) + personal thank you
Week 1: Proactive onboarding, ensure first value moment fast
Day 30: "How are you feeling about the decision?" (embrace the question)
Month 2-3: Request review/testimonial (after measurable success)
Month 3-4: Referral ask: "Who else in your network faces this problem?"
Ongoing: Quarterly value reports, keep them feeling smart about their decision
```
